How this guide is organised

This guide covers digital marketing from zero to production. Every chapter follows the same rhythm: a topic description, prose explanation, two or more real-world code samples (CSV, JSON, HTML, or JS), and a callout flagging the most common mistake.

Structure of every section

Each h3 section is self-contained: a topic-desc intro, prose explanation, two code samples demonstrating real setup or measurement, and a tip, warn, or note callout. Chapters 1–3 build the foundation: strategy, ICP, funnels, and research. Chapters 4–7 cover the main channels: content, SEO (overview only), paid search, and social. The remaining chapters go deep on paid social, email, CRO, analytics, budget, retention, MarTech, and the 90-day plan.

The mindset: funnels over blasts

Digital marketing is not posting and hoping. It is a systematic process of driving the right people through a measurable funnel, then iterating on every step.

Measure before you optimise

Every chapter points at a real metric: open rate, click-through, cost per acquisition, customer lifetime value, return on ad spend. Set up measurement first, run your campaign, and re-measure. Without data, you are guessing.

Sequence matters

Do not start with paid ads before you know your audience. Do not write content before you have a distribution channel. Do not build an email list before you have an offer worth subscribing to. This guide orders the channels deliberately: strategy first, distribution second, optimisation last.

Tools you will use throughout

You can do real marketing with a free stack. Google Analytics tracks behaviour, Google Search Console shows search performance, Mailchimp’s free tier sends emails, and a free Buffer account schedules posts. Paid tools earn their keep at scale, but the workflow is the same.

A typical free stack

Google Analytics 4 for traffic and conversion tracking. Google Search Console for SEO performance. Google Trends for demand signals. Mailchimp for email sequences. Meta Business Suite for paid social. YouTube Studio for video performance. All free, all sufficient for most small to mid-sized projects.

Pick one primary KPI per channel and check it weekly. Opening every dashboard every day is a distraction, not a habit.

1.1 Building a strategy

A marketing strategy is a written plan: who you are targeting, what offer you are pushing, which channels you will use, and how you will measure success. Without it, every tactic is a guess.

A one-page strategy template

# Marketing Strategy — Q4 2026

## Target audience
Small business owners, 25–45, who sell services locally.

## Core offer
A 12-week SEO growth plan at $800/month.

## Channels
1. Organic content (blog + YouTube) — 40% of effort
2. Email newsletter — 30%
3. Local sponsorships — 20%
4. Paid search — 10%

## Primary KPI
50 qualified leads per month, measured in HubSpot.

## Budget
$2,000/month. 60% ads, 25% tools, 15% content production.

1.2 ICP, positioning & value proposition

ICP stands for Ideal Customer Profile. It is the specific person (or company) most likely to buy your offer. Positioning is how you describe your offer so that person chooses you over alternatives.

Writing your value proposition

For [target audience],
who [needs or pain],
our product [does what],
so that [benefit].

Example: "For local plumbers who lose leads to Google Ads, our service delivers qualified calls from organic search so that they stop paying per click."

1.3 The funnel: TOFU, MOFU, BOFU

The funnel is a model of how a stranger becomes a customer. TOFU (Top of Funnel) is awareness. MOFU (Middle) is consideration. BOFU (Bottom) is decision.

Mapping content to funnel stages

TOFU — blog posts, YouTube videos, infographics.
MOFU — comparison guides, case studies, webinars.
BOFU — free trial, consultation call, demo.

Example: a visitor reads a TOFU post on "what is SEO".
Next week they receive a MOFU email comparing SEO services.
The following week they book a BOFU consultation call.

2.1 Surveys, interviews & jobs-to-be-done

Research means talking to real people. Surveys scale; interviews go deep. Jobs-to-be-done is a framework for understanding the outcome customers really want.

Interview script

1. Tell me about the last time you looked for [product/service].
2. What did you search for? Which websites did you visit?
3. What did you like or dislike about each option?
4. What would make you choose one over another?
5. If you could change one thing, what would it be?

2.2 Competitor teardowns

A competitor teardown forces you to look at how others market and where they leave gaps you can fill.

Teardown checklist

1. Visit their website. What is the first thing you see?
2. Check their page speed with PageSpeed Insights.
3. Look at their blog: which topics get the most traffic?
4. Check their social media: which posts get the most engagement?
5. Read their reviews: what do people praise or complain about?
6. Note one thing they do well and one thing you could do better.

2.3 Customer voice of the customer

Voice of the customer (VoC) means using the exact words your audience uses in reviews, forums, and support tickets. These phrases belong in your headlines, ads, and emails.

Finding authentic phrases

// Amazon reviews for a similar product
"best value for the price"
"easy to set up, works straight out of the box"
"wish it had a longer cord"

// Your ad headline:
"The budget espresso machine that just works."
